P-120 · DYADIC RESEARCH LAYER

Are we together?

Dyadic Research Architecture
Not how similar you are. What happens between you.

Comparing two profiles is easy. Giving that comparison a percentage is even easier. Neither of those things is the same as measuring compatibility.

Two very different people may adapt to one another extremely well. Two similar people may struggle to discuss differences, amplify tension, or fail to recover after conflict and mismatch. The P-120 dyadic layer therefore does not reduce a relationship to one “Couple Score”. It studies several distinct levels of how a specific pair functions.

TWO INDEPENDENT SYSTEMS

First — two independent systems

Each person keeps their own P-120 profile and, where completed, their own additional research layers. Neither partner becomes the other person’s “correct half”, and no individual score becomes a rating of relationship quality.

FOUR-LEVEL COMPATIBILITY DOCTRINE

Compatibility is not one number

I

Structural Fit

Which two systems met?

Individual profiles, differences and potentially relevant points of contact.

II

Negotiated Fit

What do two people do with their differences?

Can they notice mismatch, communicate needs, adapt, negotiate and repair contact?

III

Experienced Dynamic Fit

What does the interaction actually do to each person’s state?

Does it produce mutual recovery, one-sided relief, load, co-regulation or another reproducible state pattern?

IV

Temporal Stability

Does the pattern repeat over time?

A single episode should not automatically become a characteristic of the entire relationship.

KEY RULE. Structural similarity is not compatibility. Structural mismatch can coexist with good adaptation; structural similarity can coexist with poor repair and co-dysregulation.
DYADIC PROCESS

Sometimes the break occurs somewhere else than it seems

NEED
PERCEPTION
PROVISION
EXPERIENCE
EFFECT

A person may need a particular form of contact. A partner may understand that need correctly but provide something different from what was expected. Or one person may believe they delivered exactly what was needed while the other experiences the interaction in a completely different way.

Dyadic architecture is designed to locate the break: need, perception, action, experience or subsequent effect, instead of immediately applying a global label of “compatible / incompatible”.
DYNAMIC RESEARCH ENGINES

Two trajectories instead of one “couple score”

SCORE-D

What intimacy does to each person’s state

Sexual Co-Regulation & Recovery

SCORE-D studies changes in state around intimate contact and repeated interactions: stress, affect, recovery, subjective resource, interdependence and possible asymmetry of recovery.

The central question is not whether there is “energy” between two people, but how two independently measured states change over time.

Synchrony ≠ co-regulation ≠ compatibility
Research Candidate · controlled pre-pilot architecture
DESIRE-D

Desire moves through time too

Dyadic Desire & Erotic Coordination

Desire in a relationship cannot be reduced correctly to two fixed “libido levels”. DESIRE-D is being developed as two independent trajectories: one’s own desire, perception of the partner’s desire, signals, interpretation of mismatch, scenario change, and how desire moves within specific interactions.

No libido compatibility score · No desire compatibility percentage · No Couple Total
Candidate measurement architecture · in development
PRIVACY BY DESIGN

Studying two people should not take away either person’s private space

Dyadic research is meaningful only if each person can answer independently and is not required to disclose their individual responses to the partner.

C1Individual participation

Each person participates independently and controls their own responses.

C2Pseudonymous pairing

Linking two participation records requires separate consent.

C3Share-safe report consent

Only mutually authorised interpretation that is safe to disclose.

C4Optional event study

Separate permission for repeated event-level research.

Partners complete the assessment separately.
One partner’s raw answers are not shown to the other.
A shared report does not expose sensitive safety data.
An optional question may be skipped and a sensitive flow may be stopped.
Shared-report consent can be withdrawn.
The interface does not tell a partner which sensitive steps the other skipped or stopped.
FUTURE RESULT ARCHITECTURE

A relationship result should not be one percentage either

The structure below is a preview of a future architecture, not an already available production report.

01 / INDIVIDUAL

My state

How the interaction is associated with my own state.

02 / PARTNER-CONTEXT

This partner’s context

How my states and reactions change specifically in the context of this partner — without exposing their raw answers.

03 / DYADIC

Mutual pattern

Which mutual patterns can be described safely, and only where the data and mutual consent are sufficient.

Future report architecture. Production interpretation is not yet authorised.
SCIENTIFIC BOUNDARY

What this page does not promise

No Couple Total.
No Compatibility %.
Similarity is not declared to be compatibility.
One event does not become a characteristic of the entire relationship.
Synchrony is not equated with co-regulation.
A research preview is not a validated production product.
SCORE-DControlled candidate/pre-pilot architecture; production score/report authority is not claimed.
DESIRE-DCandidate dyadic desire measurement architecture; a single libido/compatibility score is prohibited.
Dyadic privacyIndependent participation, pseudonymous pairing and share-safe disclosure remain mandatory boundaries.
