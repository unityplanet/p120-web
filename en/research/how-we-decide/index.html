P-120 RESEARCH · RESEARCH · IN DEVELOPMENT

How we make decisions

A research project for a human-governed cognitive analysis system

More happens between information and a decision than it may seem. We see facts, interpret them, make assumptions, treat some things as obvious, and gradually arrive at a choice. Yet the path to the decision itself usually remains almost invisible.

We are investigating whether that path can be made observable and reviewable — not so that a system decides for a person, but so that a person can better see what their own decision rests on.

informationjudgment
HOW THIS LOOKS IN PRACTICE

One decision. Several layers that are easy to confuse.

In a difficult decision, facts rarely stand alone. Explanations, expectations, fears and assumptions quickly accumulate around them — and after a while they can begin to feel equally certain.

HG-CGA helps separate these layers and identify what information is actually missing, without making the decision for the person.

01
RELATIONSHIPS AND A SHARED FUTURE

Should I keep building a shared future with this person?

We have been together for almost two years and have talked more than once about a shared future. My partner has repeatedly said they want us to live together, but moving in together has already been postponed twice. Most recently, they explained the delay by uncertainty around work and finances. I am beginning to think it may not be just circumstances: perhaps they are simply not ready to build a shared future with me.

FACT
The relationship has lasted almost two years. Conversations about a shared future have taken place. Moving in together was planned and has been postponed twice.
SOURCE
My partner says the latest postponement is connected to work and financial circumstances.

Important: the fact is that this explanation was given. That does not yet establish that the explanation is the true cause.

INTERPRETATION
“Repeated postponements may mean that my partner is avoiding a serious commitment or does not see our shared future in the same way I do.”
ASSUMPTION
“If someone genuinely wants a shared future, they should be ready to move in together now.”
UNKNOWN
Whether the obstacles are temporary. What conditions would need to change. Whether both people mean the same thing by “a shared future” and expect the same pace of progression.
ALTERNATIVE MODEL
The intention to build a shared future may be real, while readiness for the next concrete step is currently limited by circumstances, risk tolerance or a mismatch in pace.

This is not the system's conclusion about my partner's motives. It is only another model compatible with what is already known.

CRITICAL QUESTION
What specific action, condition or agreement within a mutually agreed timeframe would allow me to distinguish temporary unreadiness to live together from a lack of sustained intention to build a shared future?
What HG-CGA does here

It does not tell me to “stay” or “leave,” and it does not determine how sincere my partner is. It shows which parts of the decision are facts, which are interpretations, what assumptions connect them, and what new information could genuinely distinguish competing explanations.

Not every uncertainty is about another person's intentions.
Sometimes the main source of uncertainty is the future itself.

02
RELOCATION AND LIFE TRAJECTORY

Should I move to another country and change the life I know?

I have been offered a job in another country with higher pay and an opportunity for professional growth. I need to decide within the next few months. Here I have a stable life and people close to me. I am afraid both of missing a rare opportunity and of moving only to discover later that the new life does not suit me.

FACT
There is a concrete offer, a limited decision window, higher pay and a requirement to relocate.
SOURCE
The formal terms of the offer and the available information about the employer.

Important: stated professional growth is not guaranteed. Current stability also does not guarantee that life will remain unchanged.

INTERPRETATION
“If I turn this down, I may miss a rare opportunity.” At the same time: “If I move, I may lose what is already good and stable in my life.”
ASSUMPTION
“An opportunity like this will not come again.” “My current life will stay roughly the same.” “If the move is a mistake, it will be very difficult to reverse.”
UNKNOWN
Whether the new job will match expectations. Whether the new environment will suit me. Which parts of the decision are truly hard to reverse. Whether I can preserve important relationships and the option to return.
ALTERNATIVE MODEL
The choice may not be completely binary: some uncertainty may be reducible through additional information or by making part of the decision more reversible.

HG-CGA does not claim that such an option exists in every case or that it is the better choice.

CRITICAL QUESTION
What additional information, or what possibility of partial reversibility, would most change my willingness to move or stay?
What HG-CGA does here

It does not choose between “stay” and “move.” It separates actual conditions from forecasts about the future and shows what could genuinely change the decision.

The same structure remains when the decision becomes professional or operational.

03
OPERATIONAL DECISION · TRANSFERABILITY

The same architecture in a different domain

The delivery was nine days late. The supplier says it was a logistics problem, but I think the issue is their reliability.

FACT
The delivery was nine days late.
SOURCE
The supplier attributes the delay to logistics. The fact that this explanation was given does not establish that it is the true cause.
INTERPRETATION
“The supplier may be unreliable.”
ASSUMPTION
The cause of the delay was under the supplier's control.
UNKNOWN
Whether external logistics disruptions occurred that the supplier could not prevent.
ALTERNATIVE MODEL
The supplier may have acted correctly while the delay arose outside their control. This is a competing explanation, not an exoneration.
CRITICAL QUESTION
What information would distinguish a failure by the supplier from an external logistics cause?

HG-CGA does not decide whether to replace the supplier. It shows where the fact ends and the reliability inference begins — and what information is needed before making that decision.

DIFFERENT SITUATIONS · ONE COGNITIVE TASK

Different domains. The same structural problem.

Relationships, relocation and an operational decision look very different. But inside each one the same task reappears:

separate what was observed from how it is explained, expose hidden assumptions, keep unknowns unknown, and identify the question that could genuinely change the decision.

HG-CGA does not remove uncertainty or turn a difficult choice into an automatic answer. It makes the structure of the decision more explicit: what is known, where it comes from, what is already an interpretation, what assumptions are being used and what information is still missing.

HUMAN AUTHORITY

The decision remains human.

HG-CGA does not substitute for the human decision. It makes the structure of the decision more explicit, reviewable and governable — after which the final choice remains with the person.

04
CAPABILITIES UNDER STUDY

What we want the system to be able to do

Distinguish

Facts, inferences, assumptions and uncertainty — without collapsing them into one layer.

Challenge

Contradictions, competing explanations, red-team challenges and unknowns that could change the choice.

Preserve and revisit

Decision history, HOLD gates, review triggers and new versions without rewriting the past.

HUMAN AUTHORITY

Automate analysis — not hand over the decision

COMPUTATIONAL ANALYSIS→QUESTIONS · OPTIONS · FLAGS→HUMAN JUDGMENT

A computational system may compare claims, detect contradictions, propose alternative models and return questions that a person has not checked. But it does not automatically acquire the authority to determine truth, goals, values or the final decision.

05
WHAT WE WANT TO TEST

Compare ways of reasoning on the same tasks

The central research question is not whether a computational system can “think better than a person.” We want to test whether the decision process can be made more transparent, reviewable and robust to particular classes of error without taking final judgment away from the person.
