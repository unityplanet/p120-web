From the Creator — P-120

From the Creator's Notes

P-120 did not begin with a test.

It began with a question.

01 / The Question

Why can a person feel so much — and still have so little language for it?

Why can two people be close — and still fail to truly tell each other who they are?

02 / Life Came First

I thought about this for many years. Not as a researcher who sat down one day before a blank page and decided to create a new test.

Life came first. Relationships. Mistakes. Encounters. Separations. Periods of closeness — and years of solitude, when there is much more time to look back, compare, and ask uncomfortable questions.

I spoke with very different people — people who explain the human being through psychology, philosophy, the body, feelings, or intuition. And the more explanations I heard, the less interested I became in what a person is supposed to be.

What interested me was something else: what does a person actually experience when no one is asking for the right answer?

03 / Between Feeling and Language

A person can have a feeling — but no word for it.

Desire — but no inner permission to speak about it.

A bodily response — but no understanding of what it means specifically for them.

Sometimes the distance between experience and language becomes greater than the distance between two people.

04 / What Remains Unsaid

CLOSENESSWhat makes me move closer?
DISTANCEWhat makes me step back?
TOUCHWhat does touch mean to me?
FREEDOMWhen does freedom become distance?
DESIREWhat do I recognize as an expression of desire?
LANGUAGEWhat do I expect from another person but never put into words?
INNER EXPERIENCEWhat do I feel but still do not know how to name?

05 / Observation

So much between people falls apart not because they feel nothing.

Sometimes because they feel more than they know how to translate into words.

Something left unsaid does not always mean concealment. Sometimes a person has not yet seen the structure of what is happening inside them.

At some point another question emerged:

What if, before explaining a person, we simply asked them sufficiently precise questions?

Not whether they are a good partner. Not whether their desires are normal. Not who they ought to be.

But how, specifically, they experience closeness, desire, touch, initiative, safety, distance, and reciprocity.

A philosophical question becomes a research task.

07 / Coordinates

That is how P-120 gradually emerged.

Not as a formula for a person. Not as a final verdict.

As a coordinate system.

Complexity does not become simple when it gains coordinates. It becomes discernible.

That was what interested me: not reducing a person to a result, but increasing the resolution with which they can look at their own experience.

08 / Principles

NOT A LABELBUT VISIBILITY.
NOT A GRADEBUT LANGUAGE.
NOT ONE SCOREBUT LAYERS.
NOT A DEFECTBUT DIFFERENCE.
NOT A PROPHECYBUT REFLECTION.
NOT MYSTIFICATIONBUT GROUNDS.

09 / Where the Author Ends

P-120 was born from a personal and philosophical question.

But the system's answers should not depend on its creator's philosophy.

An observation must become a hypothesis. A hypothesis — a defined construct. A measurement — something that can be tested. And where the evidence is insufficient, the system must be able to say: “We do not know this yet.”

To me, that is not a weakness. It is the boundary between conviction and knowledge.

P-120 should not read minds, “reveal the subconscious,” or determine fate. Its task is to operate only within what can actually be justified.

10 / P-120 North Star

That is why P-120's task is at once more modest and more difficult.

Not to explain a person instead of them.
Not to define them.
Not to fix them.

Help them notice.

Make the inner visible.

Sometimes the first real step toward understanding another person begins not with a conversation with them, but with the moment we have looked at ourselves closely enough.
